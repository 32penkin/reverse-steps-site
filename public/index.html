How it works

  1. Photo first, then unscrewStart a project, shoot how the thing looks now, then take a photo before each change. Steps are numbered for you.
  2. Add parts and a notePhotograph the screws and brackets you removed, record “two short screws, cable clip up” by text or voice.
  3. Reassemble in reverseSwitch to reassembly: full-screen cards from the last step to the first. Tick each one off and compare with the starting photo at the end.

Why not just the camera roll?

Steps, not a photo pile

Each project keeps its own numbered steps, apart from your other photos.

Parts belong to a step

The screws you removed are attached to the step they came from, with your note.

Order is reversed for you

No scrolling back through the roll and guessing what came first.

Private by default

Photos, voice notes and notes stay on the device. Export a ZIP or save to your gallery whenever you want.

Optional reassembly hints

If you turn them on, the app suggests a short caption for each step from your own photos. They can be wrong, so the photo is always shown next to them.

What it does not do

Reverse Steps records what you did; it does not teach repairs or guarantee a correct reassembly. Follow the manufacturer’s instructions, switch off and unplug the power, and do not open batteries, gas appliances or high-voltage equipment.

Where photos are stored, export, free teardowns: see Support.

Reverse Steps keeps a record of your own steps. It is not a repair manual and does not check that a repair is safe or correct.
